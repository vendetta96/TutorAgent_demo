# Volcanoes

A volcano is an opening in the Earth's crust where melted rock, ash, and gases escape. Melted rock underground is called magma; once it flows out onto the surface it is called lava. Most volcanoes form where tectonic plates pull apart or push together, and some, like the Hawaiian islands, form over hot spots in the middle of a plate.

## Why volcanoes erupt

Magma is lighter than the solid rock around it, so it rises. Gas dissolved in the magma, like carbon dioxide and water vapour, forms bubbles as the magma rises, a bit like opening a shaken fizzy drink. If the magma is runny, the gas escapes gently and lava flows out. If the magma is thick and sticky, pressure builds up until the volcano erupts explosively, throwing out ash and rock.

## Types of volcanoes

Shield volcanoes are wide and gently sloping, built from runny lava; Mauna Loa in Hawaii is a shield volcano. Stratovolcanoes, also called composite volcanoes, are tall and cone-shaped with steep sides, built from layers of ash and lava; Mount Fuji in Japan and Mount Vesuvius in Italy are stratovolcanoes. Cinder cones are small, steep hills built from small pieces of erupted rock.

A volcano is active if it has erupted recently or is expected to erupt again, dormant if it is quiet but could wake up, and extinct if scientists think it will never erupt again. There are around 1,500 potentially active volcanoes on land in the world.

## Volcano hazards and safety

The main dangers from volcanoes are lava flows, ash clouds that can damage lungs and aircraft engines, fast-moving clouds of hot gas and ash called pyroclastic flows, and mudflows called lahars. Scientists called volcanologists watch volcanoes using instruments that measure ground swelling, small earthquakes, and gas, so they can warn people to evacuate before an eruption. If ash is falling, stay indoors, close windows, and wear a mask if you must go outside.

Volcanoes are not only destructive. Volcanic soil is very fertile, which is why many people farm near volcanoes, and volcanic heat is used to make geothermal electricity in countries like Iceland and New Zealand.

# Landslides

A landslide is when rock, earth, or debris slides down a slope. Landslides are often triggered by heavy rain, which makes soil heavy and slippery, or by earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and people cutting away the bottom of hillsides for roads or buildings. Removing trees from slopes makes landslides more likely because tree roots help hold soil together.

Signs of a possible landslide include new cracks in the ground, tilting trees or poles, and doors and windows that suddenly stick. During heavy rain in hilly areas, stay alert and move away from steep slopes and stream channels if you hear rumbling or see moving debris. Planting trees and building retaining walls help prevent landslides.
